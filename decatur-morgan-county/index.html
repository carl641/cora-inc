Service Area

Decatur’s sewer service stops at the city line. Past it — Priceville, Somerville, Falkville, Eva, Danville, Trinity, Laceys Spring, and every county road in between — Morgan County homes run on their own septic tanks and field lines, and nobody maintains those but the people who own them.

Morgan County Residents

Morgan County lives on septic once you leave the city line

Decatur has municipal sewer, and so does much of Hartselle. The rest of the county does not. From Trinity west toward Danville and Falkville, east through Somerville, Cotaco, Union Hill, and Laceys Spring, and out along every gravel road between them, wastewater goes into a tank in the yard and out into a field line. No monthly sewer bill — and no utility crew coming when something goes wrong.

Morgan County holds a lot of long-held rural property: family land, farmhouses, and homes built well before anyone wrote down where the field lines run. We pump tanks that have not been opened in fifteen years, dig out lids buried under a decade of yard work, and give people a straight answer about whether what they have is fine, tired, or about to give out.

  • Routine pump-outs every 3–5 years for rural Morgan County households
  • Tank locating on older farm and family property with no records
  • Drain field diagnosis for standing water, odors, and slow fixtures
  • Risers and lids installed so future pump-outs are quick and clean
  • Documented inspections for county home sales and refinancing
  • 24/7 emergency response for backups and overflowing tanks

Local Conditions

What Morgan County ground does to a septic system

The valley floor around Decatur, Trinity, and Priceville is heavy clay. Clay drains slowly, which means a field line sized for it has very little patience for an overloaded tank or a wet winter. Along Wheeler Lake and the backwaters near the wildlife refuge, the water table sits high enough that fields can stay saturated for weeks. Climb east toward the Brindlee Mountain side above Laceys Spring and Union Hill and the soil thins out over rock. Head south toward Danville and Falkville and it is slope, more than soil, that decides where a field can even go.

Growth has added a second kind of customer. Subdivisions around Priceville, Hartselle, and the I-65 corridor have put new homes on ground that was pasture a few years ago, and a lot of those owners have never had a septic system before. We would rather explain how it works on a routine pump-out than meet you on the worst day of your year.

  • Heavy valley clay around Decatur and Trinity that sheds water slowly
  • High water near Wheeler Lake and the Tennessee River backwaters
  • Thin soil over rock on the Brindlee Mountain side of the county
  • Decades-old tanks on long-held family and farm property
  • Onsite sewage permits handled through the Morgan County Health Department

In the City of Decatur

And yes — we work inside Decatur too

Decatur is the commercial center of the county, and that is most of what we do inside the city limits: grease traps for restaurants and commercial kitchens, hydro jetting on drain lines that grease has narrowed down to nothing, lift station and pump repair for apartment complexes and businesses, camera inspection on sewer lines up to 8″, and hydro excavation when a line has to be exposed without hitting the utilities around it.

Homes on the outer edges of town and in older annexed pockets still sit on septic, and we service those the same way we service anything out in the county.

  • Hydro jetting for commercial drain lines and kitchen waste lines

  • Televised (TVI) camera inspection for sewer lines up to 8″
  • Hydro excavation around buried utilities

Questions

Morgan County Septic FAQs

The questions we get most often from homeowners on their own system.

How often should a Morgan County home pump its septic tank?

Every 3–5 years suits most households. Bigger families, garbage disposals, and heavy water use shorten that. Rural properties that sat vacant or changed hands recently are worth checking regardless — we will tell you what the sludge level actually says.

Our farmhouse tank has not been pumped in years. Is that a problem?

It is common out here, and it is fixable. The risk is that solids carry over into the field lines, which is a far more expensive repair than a pump-out. We locate the tank, pump it, inspect the baffles, and tell you whether the field took any damage.

Who permits a new or replacement septic system in Morgan County?

Onsite sewage systems in Alabama are permitted through the county health department — here, the Morgan County Health Department. We coordinate the permits and inspections when we install or replace a system.

Our ground stays wet all winter. Will a drain field even work?

Sometimes the answer is a repair, sometimes it is a field in a different spot, and sometimes it is a different kind of system altogether. Heavy clay and a high water table are exactly the conditions that decide it. We look at the site before we tell you what it needs.

Do you cover the whole county?

We do — Decatur and Hartselle out to Falkville, Eva, Somerville, and Laceys Spring. We also cover Madison and Limestone Counties, plus Lawrence County and parts of southern Tennessee.
